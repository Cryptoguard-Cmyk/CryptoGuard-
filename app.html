01 / THE BASICS5 MIN READ

Your recovery phrase is not a password to share.

A recovery phrase can give someone control over a crypto wallet. Treat it like the most sensitive key to that wallet: keep it private and never send it to a person, website or support agent.

Keep this in mind

  • Do not type your phrase into a website because a message tells you to.
  • Do not share it with someone claiming they need it to fix a problem.
  • Be cautious of screenshots, cloud notes and messages that could expose it.

The simple rule: anyone asking you to reveal your recovery phrase should be treated as untrusted.

02 / SCAM AWARENESS4 MIN READ

That message from “support” may not be support.

Scammers sometimes pretend to work for an exchange, wallet provider or project. They may contact you after you post a question publicly, then try to move the conversation into private messages.

Pause when a message

  • Arrives unexpectedly and claims your account is in danger.
  • Pressures you to act immediately or keep the conversation secret.
  • Asks for passwords, one-time codes, private keys or recovery phrases.
  • Sends a link and tells you to sign in to solve an urgent issue.

A safer habit: close the message and contact the company using contact details from its official website or app that you navigate to yourself.

03 / WALLET SAFETY6 MIN READ

Before you approve a wallet transaction.

A wallet prompt is not just a button to get past. Depending on the action, you may be authorizing a transfer or granting an application permission to interact with certain tokens.

Take a moment to check

  • Do you recognize the website or application?
  • Do you understand what the wallet says you are approving?
  • Does the requested action match what you intended to do?
  • Can you verify the request using the project's official documentation?

If it is unclear, do not approve it just to make the warning disappear. Seek help from a trusted, independently verified source.
